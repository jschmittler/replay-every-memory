# Trash Dash
## Full Story Narrative

### Prologue: A Normal Morning

Trashy had never considered himself a hero.

He was a raccoon.

A particularly curious raccoon, certainly. A raccoon with an excellent eye for forgotten treasures, an unmatched appreciation for yesterday's leftovers, and very strong opinions about what did and did not qualify as good trash.

But still, just a raccoon.

Trashy lived high in the tallest tree of a quiet forest. His home was filled with things other creatures had thrown away: broken toys, old tools, shiny buttons, mysterious keys, pieces of machines he did not understand, and dozens of other objects that Trashy was certain would become useful eventually.

Most mornings began the same way.

This one began with a growl.

Trashy's eyes opened.

Another growl.

He looked down at his stomach.

"Okay. I get it."

Breakfast.

Trashy searched his food stash.

Empty.

The next container was empty too.

The third held one lonely crumb.

Trashy picked it up, inspected it, smelled it, and put it back.

Even Trashy had standards.

He climbed down from his home and stepped into the morning forest looking for something better.

Nearby sat an old metal trash can beneath another tree.

It shook.

Trashy stopped.

The trash can shook harder.

Trashy sighed.

The lid exploded into the air.

"TRASHY!"

Jimothy emerged triumphantly.

Jimothy was Trashy's best friend, neighbor, and possibly the strangest creature in the entire forest. He was lankier than Trashy, considerably less cautious, and possessed an enthusiasm for pressing buttons, opening containers, climbing things, jumping from things, and eating things that would have concerned almost anyone else.

"I have exciting news," Jimothy announced.

"What happened?"

Jimothy proudly held up a spoon.

"I found a spoon."

Trashy stared at it.

"That's the exciting news?"

Jimothy looked offended.

"Look at it."

Trashy smiled.

It should have been another normal morning.

It wasn't.

---

# Chapter 1: Woodlands to City Limits

Trashy first noticed the silence.

The birds had stopped singing.

Something rushed through the undergrowth nearby. A normally harmless forest creature appeared between the trees, agitated and aggressive.

Then Trashy saw the stain.

Purple and black.

Thick.

Oily.

Wrong.

It clung to roots and stones in heavy smears, as though someone had spilled rotten tar across the forest floor.

Jimothy leaned toward it.

"Trash?"

"No."

Jimothy looked at him.

Trashy didn't know how he knew.

He simply did.

"That's the problem."

A rabbit hopped into the clearing.

It noticed the purple-black sludge.

Trashy watched as it sniffed the substance.

The rabbit seemed fascinated by it.

"Wait..."

It tasted the Gunk.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then the rabbit twitched.

Its movements became unnatural. Its body began changing. Familiar features exaggerated and distorted as the harmless forest animal transformed into something monstrous.

Trashy stared in horror.

Jimothy took one slow step backward.

"Okay."

Another step.

"That's bad trash."

When the creature attacked, Trashy had no choice but to defend himself.

He fought until the animal collapsed.

Trashy cautiously approached.

He expected the transformation to reverse.

It didn't.

The creature was breathing.

Alive.

Still mutated.

"Come on..."

Nothing.

Jimothy crouched beside him.

"Maybe it needs a nap."

Trashy kept staring.

"A really long nap?"

Trashy stood.

Whatever was happening, fighting the animals wasn't fixing it.

And suddenly he thought about everyone else in the forest.

The two followed the contamination deeper through the woods.

The Gunk appeared along tree roots, around discarded food, beside the creek, beneath an old ruined mill. Small traces collected along stones and water channels.

It wasn't staying in one place.

It was traveling.

As they crossed the ruined bridge and climbed toward the edge of the forest, human structures began appearing.

Drainage pipes.

Fences.

Guardrails.

A highway.

Beyond them, for the first time in Trashy's life, he saw the distant lights of the city.

Jimothy stared.

"That's a lot of lights."

Trashy looked down.

Another streak of Gunk disappeared through a culvert toward civilization.

"And the Gunk is going right toward them."

The forest eventually gave way to warehouses, rail cars, shipping containers, and drainage channels.

There was more contamination here.

Much more.

Then Trashy saw something familiar.

Trash.

A beautiful pile of it.

Jimothy's eyes widened.

"Finally."

"Don't."

"I didn't do anything."

The pile moved.

Jimothy slowly lowered his paws.

The trash rose into the air, fused together by the purple-black substance into a massive corrupted creature.

Trashy had spent his whole life loving forgotten things.

Now one of them wanted to eat him.

The battle was long, but the two raccoons eventually brought the creature crashing down.

Again, the Gunk remained.

Again, nothing was cured.

Trashy looked toward the neighborhood ahead.

"We stopped it."

He looked at the defeated monster.

"But we didn't fix it."

The Gunk trail continued into the streets beyond.

Jimothy looked toward the city.

"So..."

"More trash?"

Trashy started walking.

"More answers."

Jimothy followed.

Of course he did.

---

# Chapter 2: Suburban After Dark

To Jimothy, suburbia was magnificent.

Trash cans lined the streets.

Garbage bags sat beside driveways.

Humans apparently discarded useful things everywhere.

Jimothy slowly turned in a circle, taking it all in.

"Trashy."

"Don't."

"You don't even know what I was going to say."

"I do."

"I was going to scream."

Before he could, an acorn struck him in the head.

Jimothy blinked.

Another flew past Trashy.

Above them, an enraged squirrel prepared another attack.

"Neighborhood squirrels are mean," Jimothy said.

Trashy noticed purple-black stains around the tree.

"No."

He looked at the squirrel.

"They're sick."

The forest wasn't the only place affected.

The Gunk had spread throughout the neighborhood.

Trashy and Jimothy followed overturned bins, contaminated garbage, greasy stains along wet pavement, and thick residue collecting in storm drains.

At one point Jimothy spotted an especially promising garbage bag.

He hurried toward it.

Stopped.

Sniffed.

Recoiled.

"Absolutely not."

Trashy looked at him.

"You ate a shoe yesterday."

"It was a good shoe."

Neither raccoon completely understood why the Gunk repulsed them when every other animal seemed irresistibly attracted to it.

But Trashy trusted his instincts.

Something about it was pretending to be trash.

And Trashy knew better.

The deeper they traveled through the neighborhood, the clearer the trail became.

The Gunk was moving through drainage systems and culverts toward the city.

Then came the bark.

Deep.

Familiar.

Followed by the scrape of metal.

Trashy froze.

"Brutus?"

The creature that emerged barely resembled the neighborhood bulldog Trashy remembered.

Brutus had become enormous. His body was fused with a battered garbage can and other discarded material, transformed into a charging mass of dog, metal, muscle, and Gunk.

He snarled.

Then, for just a second, his expression changed.

Recognition.

"Trashy?"

It was barely more than a sound.

Trashy stepped forward.

"Brutus..."

Brutus whimpered.

Then his body tensed.

The corruption took control again.

"Leave."

He attacked.

Trashy finally understood.

Brutus knew what he was doing.

He simply couldn't stop himself.

The Gunk hadn't erased him.

It had hijacked him.

Whatever controlled the infection wanted Trashy and Jimothy gone.

The two couldn't overpower Brutus, so they outsmarted him.

They lured the raging bulldog through the neighborhood and toward a backyard pool.

Brutus charged.

Trashy dodged.

Brutus crashed into the water.

He thrashed furiously.

The attack stopped.

But when Trashy looked back, Brutus was still mutated.

Still trapped.

"He's still in there."

Jimothy didn't joke this time.

"Yeah."

Trashy looked toward the city.

They couldn't keep knocking their friends down and leaving them behind.

They had to find the source.

They ran.

Neither noticed how far from home they had already traveled.

---

# Chapter 3: The Big City

The city was bigger than anything Trashy had imagined.

They emerged through a restaurant alley as rain began falling.

Lights reflected across wet pavement.

Steam poured from vents.

Traffic roared beyond the buildings.

Construction echoed between towers.

And garbage was everywhere.

Jimothy stared in awe.

"Trashy..."

"No."

"You really don't know what I was going to say this time."

Trashy pointed toward the alley floor.

Purple-black Gunk coated the edges of dumpsters and disappeared beneath a sewer grate.

"It's everywhere."

"The trash?"

"The Gunk."

There was more here than anywhere else they had traveled.

Trashy began to understand.

They were getting closer.

The city frightened Jimothy in a way the forest and neighborhood hadn't.

The buildings seemed impossibly tall.

Cars moved faster than anything he understood.

The whole place felt alive.

"Trashy?"

Trashy looked at his friend.

Jimothy rarely sounded unsure.

"Are we supposed to be here?"

Trashy looked toward the trail.

"I don't know."

Then toward Jimothy.

"But we've made it this far."

They continued.

Soon they noticed something else.

Infected animals weren't attacking randomly anymore.

A group of rats watched them cross an alley.

One noticed Trashy.

Then another.

Then another.

Every head turned toward the raccoons at exactly the same moment.

Jimothy stopped.

"...Did they all just look at us?"

"Run."

The Gunk knew they were there.

Somehow, the infected creatures were connected.

The heroes climbed to the rooftops hoping to understand where the trail was leading.

From above, the city stretched endlessly in every direction.

The purple-black contamination wound through streets and alleys below.

Far away stood a strange research tower.

Trashy pointed.

"There."

Jimothy looked across the impossible distance.

"So we climb all the way over there?"

"Unless you've got a better idea."

Jimothy saw a kite tangled nearby.

"I have an idea."

Trashy immediately regretted asking.

"Jimothy."

"Too late."

Jimothy grabbed the kite and ran toward the edge.

"GERONIMOOOO!"

He jumped.

"JIMOTHY!"

Trashy grabbed another kite and followed.

For a few incredible seconds they soared between buildings.

Jimothy laughed.

Trashy almost did too.

Then a pigeon slammed into him.

A whole flock descended.

They weren't behaving like normal birds.

They attacked together.

Coordinated.

Targeted.

The heroes lost control and plunged toward the street, crashing through an open manhole.

Darkness.

Trashy groaned.

Jimothy sat up.

"Good news."

Trashy looked at him.

"We're alive."

Jimothy pointed into the dark.

"Bad news."

Dozens of eyes stared back.

Rats.

Everywhere.

A rumble began.

The rats scattered.

Trashy and Jimothy looked at one another.

A subway train exploded through the darkness, missing them by inches.

The blast sent them scrambling through maintenance tunnels until they eventually escaped back to the surface.

They emerged onto a construction site beneath the research tower.

Cranes swung overhead.

Steel beams crossed the skyline.

Gunk covered the area in thick stains.

Trashy looked toward the tower.

"That's where it ends."

Jimothy considered.

"Or begins."

Trashy stared at him.

"I don't know. It sounded important."

Then a shadow fell across them.

The smell came next.

Hot cheese.

Grease.

Pepperoni.

Jimothy sniffed.

"Is that..."

"Pizza."

A monstrous rodent stepped into view.

His Greatness.

The Pizza Rat King.

He had transformed a section of the city into his kingdom, surrounded by infected rats and cockroaches.

"Who dares disturb my kingdom?"

"We're trying to stop the Gunk," Trashy said.

The Rat King laughed.

"Stop?"

"You don't stop greatness."

The Gunk had amplified more than his body.

It had amplified ambition.

Territory.

Greed.

Intelligence.

The Rat King fought differently from Brutus.

He set traps.

Commanded followers.

Anticipated attacks.

But Trashy and Jimothy were getting better too.

They defeated him.

As the fallen king struggled to remain conscious, his arrogance disappeared for just a moment.

"I..."

"I was supposed to be more."

Trashy looked at him.

"I know."

The remaining infected creatures suddenly swarmed.

Not in revenge.

On command.

Trashy understood now.

They weren't merely following the Gunk.

The Gunk was following them.

The heroes fled into the construction infrastructure and dropped through a storm-drain entrance beneath the research tower.

The tunnel became concrete.

Then metal.

Then something else entirely.

Trashy stopped.

Machines hummed somewhere ahead.

Purple-black Gunk flowed through massive pipes.

"Someone did this."

---

# Chapter 4: Secret Space Center

The hidden facility was enormous.

Containment tanks stretched several stories high.

Machinery moved along catwalks.

Robotic arms manipulated samples.

And everywhere they looked, there was Gunk.

Jimothy sniffed.

"This place smells like bad trash."

"How bad?"

Jimothy considered.

"Scientifically bad."

Trashy stared at the enormous vats.

"This is where it's coming from."

They moved deeper into the facility.

Research terminals documented mutations.

Containment chambers held infected animals.

Recorded observations described behavioral control.

Someone had known exactly what the substance did.

"They knew," Trashy said.

"Knew what?"

"They knew what it did."

That changed something in Trashy.

Until now, everything had been a mystery.

Now he was angry.

Further inside, they discovered maps.

Purple pathways connected waste systems, drains, waterways, neighborhoods, and the forest.

Trashy stared.

"The Gunk wasn't spreading."

He looked at Jimothy.

"Someone was spreading it."

While Trashy studied the evidence, Jimothy discovered something equally fascinating.

Buttons.

Rows of them.

"Question."

"No."

"You don't know the question."

"I know you."

Jimothy pressed one.

Click.

Nothing happened.

Jimothy smiled.

Then alarms erupted.

Red lights flooded the room.

"UNAUTHORIZED SUBJECTS DETECTED."

Doors slammed shut.

Jimothy looked at Trashy.

"Good news."

Trashy waited.

"The button worked."

The facility came alive.

Security robots deployed.

Experimental creatures broke loose.

Machines turned hostile.

The heroes were chased deeper.

They crossed robotics assembly floors filled with conveyors, presses, cranes, patrol machines, and containers of packaged Gunk.

Trashy understood something even worse.

"They weren't just studying it."

Jimothy looked at the machinery.

"They were making more."

The deeper they traveled, the stranger the facility became.

Gravity disappeared.

Debris floated through enormous rotating chambers.

Magnetic rails crossed impossible spaces.

Star charts appeared on walls.

Mission equipment filled storage rooms.

Jimothy floated upside down.

"Are we still on Earth?"

"I think so."

Then they saw the rocket.

The facility wasn't just connected to the Gunk.

It was connected to space.

Eventually they reached a huge sealed door.

Across it:

**PROJECT O.P.O.S.S.U.M.**

The door opened.

A gigantic rocket hangar stretched before them.

A spacecraft dominated the center.

And beside it stood something horrifying.

A giant opossum.

Mutated.

Armored.

Integrated with machinery.

Robotic enhancements covered his body.

He stepped from the shadows.

"Interesting."

Trashy prepared himself.

"Who are you?"

The creature examined them.

"Two unaffected organisms."

Trashy frowned.

"You did this?"

"I improved it."

PROJECT O.P.O.S.S.U.M. knew them.

He knew how they fought.

He understood their immunity.

He had studied them.

"You should not exist," he told Trashy.

"Then tell me why."

"Earn the answer."

The battle was unlike anything before it.

O.P.O.S.S.U.M. predicted attacks.

Used machinery.

Weaponized Gunk.

Adapted.

He was stronger than the Trash Monster.

More dangerous than Brutus.

Smarter than the Rat King.

Trashy and Jimothy were nearly defeated.

Then luck intervened.

O.P.O.S.S.U.M. charged.

His reinforced tail caught in the gears of one of his own machines.

Metal shrieked.

The mechanism dragged him upward and trapped him.

Jimothy stared.

"Did..."

"Did we win?"

Trashy panted.

"I think..."

"Yeah."

Trashy approached the immobilized creature.

"Why?"

O.P.O.S.S.U.M. laughed weakly.

"Why?"

"Because it promised me everything."

Trashy's expression changed.

"Who?"

"The one who made it."

He had not created the Gunk.

Neither had the facility.

O.P.O.S.S.U.M. had been offered power in exchange for helping spread it.

"I was given everything."

His machinery began failing.

"But I was never in control."

He activated one final display.

A star map appeared.

A world far beyond Earth.

Garbagia.

"The answers are out there."

O.P.O.S.S.U.M. lost consciousness.

Trashy looked toward the rocket.

They had found the source of Earth's contamination.

But not its origin.

And their friends were still infected.

"We can't stop here."

"Good," Jimothy said.

"I already wanted to see space."

They boarded.

Trashy examined the unfamiliar controls.

"Okay."

He looked directly at Jimothy.

"Nobody touch anything."

"Absolutely."

Trashy turned away.

Click.

He slowly looked back.

Jimothy's paw rested on a glowing red button.

"Jimothy..."

"I thought you said don't touch anything."

"You touched something."

"I touched one thing."

Sirens erupted.

"LAUNCH SEQUENCE INITIATED."

"WAAAAAAAIT!"

The engines ignited.

The doors closed.

Trashy and Jimothy were launched toward the stars.

---

# Chapter 5: Raccoon in Space

Earth became smaller behind them.

For once, Jimothy was quiet.

Trashy stared through the window at the world they had left.

Their forest was somewhere down there.

So was Brutus.

The Rat King.

Every infected animal they had failed to cure.

Jimothy finally spoke.

"So..."

"Are we lost?"

"Very."

"Cool."

Their spacecraft traveled deeper into space.

The first Gunk stain appeared on a damaged satellite.

Trashy froze.

"It followed us."

Jimothy looked around at the debris.

"Or..."

"It was already here."

That possibility was worse.

They traveled through a graveyard of satellites, destroyed capsules, abandoned spacecraft, and wreckage from places they didn't recognize.

The Gunk had touched other worlds.

Earth wasn't first.

They eventually reached a massive asteroid-mining network.

Drills carved through alien rock.

Old transport systems sat abandoned.

Containers bore unfamiliar markings.

The operation had once harvested something here.

Something connected to the Gunk.

Beyond the mine waited an enormous abandoned alien station.

Its technology responded strangely to the raccoons.

Doors opened before Trashy touched them.

Interfaces activated.

Symbols appeared.

Some looked disturbingly familiar.

Raccoon-like.

Inside, the heroes finally discovered the story of Garbagia.

Long ago, the inhabitants of the planet had attempted something extraordinary.

They wanted infinite trash.

Their civilization believed discarded things represented unlimited resources. If garbage could be created endlessly, Garbagia could prosper forever.

The experiment failed.

Catastrophically.

Instead of generating infinite waste, it created a purple-black biological sludge that could identify the presence of trash, infiltrate ecosystems, influence living creatures, and connect infected hosts.

The Gunk.

Most Garbagians considered the experiment a disaster.

One did not.

Galactogobbler.

He saw opportunity.

Galactogobbler already believed that trash was the greatest treasure in existence.

He also believed everyone else was wasting it.

The Gunk could destabilize a civilization.

Animals became monsters.

Infrastructure collapsed.

Chaos spread.

While worlds struggled to survive, Galactogobbler arrived and collected what he wanted.

Their trash.

Over countless journeys he accumulated more and more.

Ships.

Machines.

Structures.

Waste from entire civilizations.

He surrounded himself with it.

Added it to himself.

Grew.

Eventually he became something enormous.

A creature living inside a shell of stolen worlds.

Trashy stared at the records.

Then another piece of information appeared.

Something about Earth.

Something about raccoons.

Trashy leaned closer.

Ancient records.

Images.

Shapes resembling raccoon markings.

Technology responding specifically to them.

There was no complete explanation.

Only a possibility.

Raccoons might not have originated on Earth.

Perhaps they had been travelers.

Explorers.

Guardians.

Creatures designed to survive wherever civilizations created discarded things.

Trashy suddenly understood why the Gunk had never tempted them.

Other animals smelled the trace of trash inside it.

Trashy and Jimothy smelled something pretending to be trash.

Their entire lives had trained them to know the difference.

Trashy's obsession had never been useless.

It had saved him.

And maybe it was something much older than he understood.

The final signal led to an intergalactic junk world.

At first, Jimothy thought it was paradise.

Mountains of machinery.

Broken spacecraft.

Alien tools.

Discarded technology from worlds across the galaxy.

Then Trashy noticed what was missing.

Nothing was being used.

Nothing was appreciated.

Nothing had meaning.

Everything was simply owned.

The planet belonged to Galactogobbler.

The mountain moved.

Trashy stared upward.

It wasn't a mountain.

Galactogobbler emerged.

Huge.

Ancient.

Surrounded by the accumulated garbage of countless civilizations.

He looked down at the two raccoons.

"So."

"The ones I cannot control."

Trashy stepped forward.

"You did this."

"Of course."

"Why?"

Galactogobbler seemed almost confused by the question.

"Because nobody appreciates trash like I do."

Trashy looked across the planet.

Mountains of forgotten objects stretched beyond sight.

"You don't appreciate any of this."

Galactogobbler bristled.

"I collected it."

"That's not the same thing."

Galactogobbler laughed.

"You and I are the same."

Trashy shook his head.

"No."

"We couldn't be more different."

The fight began.

Trashy quickly discovered Galactogobbler couldn't be defeated by strength.

He was simply too massive.

Every attack seemed meaningless.

But Trashy understood something the alien did not.

Trash.

Galactogobbler couldn't resist it.

He needed to possess anything he believed valuable.

Trashy formed a plan.

He and Jimothy gathered something irresistible.

The perfect collection.

Something Galactogobbler could never allow anyone else to have.

They baited him.

Galactogobbler followed.

Through the junk world.

Across abandoned structures.

Toward their spacecraft.

The heroes ran inside.

Galactogobbler followed the treasure.

At the last possible moment Trashy and Jimothy slipped out.

The doors closed behind Galactogobbler.

Trashy triggered the launch sequence.

The spacecraft blasted away from the planet.

Galactogobbler was trapped inside.

If they could send him far enough into deep space, his telepathic control of the Gunk would break.

Earth would be free.

Trashy watched the ship disappear.

"We did it."

Jimothy smiled strangely.

"Yep."

Trashy recognized that expression.

"What did you do?"

Jimothy produced a small remote control.

A large red button sat in the center.

Trashy's eyes widened.

"Jimothy."

"I didn't trust him."

"What did you do?"

"I added fireworks."

"You added what?"

"Fireworks."

Trashy stared.

Jimothy pressed the button.

Far away, their spacecraft exploded.

A brilliant flash filled the sky.

Trashy watched in complete disbelief.

Jimothy's mouth fell open.

"Ooooooh."

The explosion faded.

Galactogobbler was gone.

# The End

Across Earth, something changed.

The Gunk lost its mind.

Without Galactogobbler, the hive connection collapsed.

The thick purple-black contamination dried.

Cracked.

Broke apart.

In the forest, infected animals fell unconscious.

Their mutations began reversing.

In suburbia, Brutus stirred.

The massive garbage-can form receded.

A normal bulldog crawled from the remains.

Wet.

Confused.

Still extremely unhappy about the pool.

In the city, the Pizza Rat King shrank back into an ordinary rat.

A slice of pizza sat beside him.

He looked at it.

Then ate it.

The forest healed.

The neighborhoods became quiet.

The city recovered.

Trashy's friends were safe.

Far away, Trashy watched the distant stars.

"We did it."

Jimothy sat beside him.

"Yeah."

Trashy smiled.

Then his expression changed.

Their spaceship was gone.

Earth was unimaginably far away.

"We're not going home."

Jimothy looked toward his friend.

Trashy stared into space.

For the first time since the adventure began, there was nowhere left to run.

But somewhere inside, he felt something else.

Pride.

He had saved them.

All of them.

Even if they never knew what he had done.

Trashy had started the adventure because he was hungry.

Now he understood there were things more important than breakfast.

More important than treasure.

More important than trash.

Home.

His friends.

Jimothy bumped into him.

Trashy looked over.

Jimothy smiled.

Well.

One friend was still here.

The screen faded.

# Epilogue: Where, But Not When

Trashy and Jimothy wandered the alien junk world.

Without Galactogobbler, it felt strangely empty.

They searched for anything that might help them return to Earth.

Eventually they found a small building buried among ancient debris.

Inside was something neither understood.

A pod.

Small.

Old.

Two seats.

Exactly large enough for two raccoons.

Trashy stared at it.

"That's weird."

Jimothy climbed inside.

"Very weird."

Trashy noticed symbols carved into the machine.

Familiar shapes.

The same raccoon-like markings from the alien station.

He reached toward them.

Behind him:

Click.

Trashy's eyes widened.

"Jimothy."

"Yes?"

"What did you press?"

"The button."

"What button?"

"The button-shaped button."

The pod activated.

Light surrounded them.

Trashy lunged for the controls.

"WAIT!"

The world disappeared.

---

Trashy woke to the sound of birds.

Grass beneath him.

Wind.

Blue sky.

Earth.

His eyes widened.

"Jimothy."

Jimothy sat up.

Trashy smiled.

"Home."

For the first time in what felt like forever, everything smelled familiar.

Plants.

Dirt.

Old metal.

Trash.

Jimothy sniffed.

"Really old trash."

Trashy's smile faded.

He looked around.

Massive brick structures stood nearby.

Broken gates.

Collapsed steel.

Vegetation covered everything.

No people.

No cars.

No city sounds.

They walked through the ruins.

Old concession stands.

Broken counters.

Cups and containers buried beneath centuries of dust.

Rows of deteriorating seats appeared ahead.

Trashy climbed.

At the top, the entire structure revealed itself.

A baseball stadium.

Abandoned.

The field below had become wild grass.

Ivy consumed the outfield walls.

The scoreboard was partially collapsed.

Beyond the stadium stood the remains of a city reclaimed by nature.

Jimothy slowly looked around.

"Trashy..."

Trashy found something still functioning inside the damaged scoreboard.

A date.

He stared.

Checked it again.

Impossible.

Thousands of years.

Trashy looked toward the ruined skyline.

"We're home."

Jimothy smiled uncertainly.

"Good?"

Trashy slowly shook his head.

"We found our way back to where."

He looked across the ancient stadium.

"But not our way back to when."

Jimothy considered this.

"So..."

"Another adventure?"

Trashy looked at his best friend.

Then at the strange future stretching beyond the stadium walls.

A small smile returned.

"Looks like it."
