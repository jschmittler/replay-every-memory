# Replay Every Memory Studios — Website Research Brief

Date: August 25, 2026  
Research source: Mobbin web-section references plus a responsive audit of the current local site  
Scope: Homepage, Trash Dash, Studio, Journal, and Press

## Executive summary

Replay Every Memory already has the hardest part: a recognizable visual identity, a memorable point of view, strong editorial copy, and a coherent flagship game. The main opportunity is not to add more design. It is to make the existing design behave more like a game-studio website.

The current homepage asks visitors to read before it lets them experience the game. It contains strong material, but the sequence is long and several sections repeat the same promise in different language. On mobile, the first viewport contains the logo, description, release status, and two calls to action, while the character/game visual begins below the fold. The supporting pages maintain the visual system, but the Journal is still an empty promise and the Studio page introduces an idea more clearly than it introduces the people.

The recommended direction is a game-first homepage with six purposeful sections, a populated development feed, a more human Studio page, and a press hub that behaves like a downloadable working resource.

## What the Mobbin references show

### 1. Art direction works best when the message stays simple

- [Unseen Studio hero](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/1bdc4e01-2f7e-4e09-9aa1-27f02c13bcbe) uses a full-canvas visual, one positioning statement, and one work CTA. The atmosphere establishes the studio before visitors read much.
- [Shader hero](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/acc902bb-ea91-4876-ad44-a2378a7c778e) turns a retro-computer metaphor into the hero and pairs it with a single proposition.
- [Deel for Gaming](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/5a5d7118-210f-41b7-8f16-947f08962f08) is not a studio site, but it demonstrates excellent category clarity: a direct headline, supporting sentence, one primary action, and a compact illustration.

Interpretation: Replay Every Memory does not need a louder hero. It needs a more experiential one. The official Trash Dash logo is strong, but the right half currently communicates the studio mark rather than the game fantasy. Gameplay, animation, or a deliberate cinematic game image should carry that side when a suitable asset is available.

### 2. Portfolio and game showcases prioritize imagery over explanation

- [Unseen Studio selected projects](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/79d328bc-800b-4830-9de1-7ef8fd2dd00f) uses a large two-column visual grid with terse titles and filters.
- [TinyWins work presentation](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/b497edad-c01e-4184-8d52-12689631b694) combines an oversized positioning line with long visual project strips and minimal metadata.
- [OFF+BRAND featured work](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/f890a5d7-f914-4f60-8ec2-a15350081db4) uses an asymmetric collage to create discovery and energy without large blocks of copy.

Interpretation: Trash Dash should be shown as a playable world, not explained as a set of principles. A studio with one announced game can still create variety through gameplay moments, characters, environments, development milestones, and journal entries.

### 3. Studio pages become credible when people and practices appear

- [Superhuman culture practices](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/31c51386-c469-4884-9e7b-5951d9216caf) names concrete rituals and pairs them with candid workplace photography.
- [HubSpot culture section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/a7f6337a-f991-42e6-820e-b45cfb23bbaf) organizes cultural attributes into digestible themes and introduces the team visually.
- [Airtable team story](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/41607641-6d7c-4795-848f-00866e74ea54) leads with mission, then explains how that belief changes how the team works.

Interpretation: “Homegrown” is a compelling studio attribute, but it should be evidenced. Visitors should see the four people, their roles, how they work together, and at least one specific development practice.

### 4. Press pages are utilities, not brand essays

- [Workable press and downloads](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/1d67ce40-c3b2-46da-a3b9-a8a750209415) places dated releases beside downloadable brand assets.
- [Qatalog press list](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/85686f39-90c9-488b-b73c-25c3f920d425) uses highly scannable rows with dates, headlines, and clear outbound indicators.
- [Grammarly press releases](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/1b85bd0f-3680-47a1-bfae-97d7cf3a718a) keeps the page intentionally plain so visitors can find and copy information quickly.

Interpretation: The current Press page has the correct intent. Its next milestone is content: a downloadable press kit, accurate fact sheet, approved logos, game screenshots, team/studio information, contact details, and dated news.

## Current-site findings

### Strengths to preserve

1. **Distinctive brand system.** The cream, navy, and gold palette is ownable and consistent across the site.
2. **Excellent voice.** Lines such as “Dreams with a start button” and “He was looking for breakfast” are memorable and specific.
3. **Clear primary property.** Visitors understand that Trash Dash is the flagship game and that it is in development for 2026.
4. **Coherent navigation.** Trash Dash, Journal, Studio, and Press are the right top-level destinations for the current stage of the company.
5. **Accessible structure.** The page has a skip link, meaningful headings, labeled navigation, descriptive image alternatives, and semantic content structure.

### Friction and risk

1. **The homepage is longer than the available proof.** Ten narrative sections make a single-game studio feel repetitive. Several sections restate values that could be shown through footage, art, or development posts.
2. **The hero visual is studio-led rather than game-led.** The running raccoon mark is clean, but it does not communicate what playing Trash Dash feels like.
3. **Mobile delays the fantasy.** At 390 × 844, the first screen shows the game logo, description, release status, and two CTAs; the character visual starts below the fold.
4. **The strongest conversion points are not ready.** “Follow Development” directs people to a Journal whose first posts are still being prepared. A prominent CTA should lead to immediate value.
5. **The Journal currently weakens momentum.** An empty editorial page makes the studio feel earlier-stage than the rest of the website.
6. **The Studio page is emotionally clear but visually anonymous.** It communicates the origin story without showing the team, roles, workspace, process, or collaboration.
7. **The Trash Dash detail-page hero does not use the official game logo.** Its large text treatment is less distinctive than the identity already available on the homepage.
8. **The press hub is directionally correct but incomplete.** It still depends on email instead of letting press and creators self-serve.

## Recommended information architecture

Keep the current top-level navigation. Simplify the homepage to this sequence:

1. **Game-first hero** — official logo, one-sentence premise, launch status, one primary CTA, one secondary CTA, and gameplay/character media.
2. **Playable proof** — trailer, gameplay loop, or intentionally designed 16:9 video thumbnail; include a duration and descriptive label.
3. **Why Trash Dash is interesting** — three highly visual moments: movement, characters, and the Gunk/world.
4. **Latest from development** — three real Journal cards with dates, categories, imagery, and short summaries.
5. **The studio behind it** — team image or illustrated portrait set, a two-sentence studio introduction, and a link to Studio.
6. **Follow/wishlist CTA and footer** — a specific action that delivers immediate value.

Move the longer principles and process manifesto to the Studio page or a dedicated Journal post. Keep only the most distinctive statement on the homepage.

## Page-level recommendations

### Homepage

- Replace the right-side hero mark with game media when a credible asset is available. Until then, use a deliberate game key art or animated character vignette rather than a generic decorative crop.
- Keep only one visually dominant CTA. If a Steam page is live, use **Wishlist on Steam**. Otherwise use **Get development updates** and make the destination valuable on arrival.
- Move the platform/release strip closer to the hero CTA and tighten its labels for fast scanning.
- Replace principle-heavy sections with image-backed evidence.
- Surface three recent Journal entries; never promote an empty feed as the primary action.

### Trash Dash

- Use the official Trash Dash logo in the page hero for cross-page recognition.
- Add a media gallery with gameplay, environments, characters, and captions.
- Give “What you do” a high position and express the core gameplay loop in three steps.
- Add a compact facts panel: genre, players, platforms, release window, developer, publisher, and languages when known.

### Journal

- Launch with at least three substantive posts before treating it as a primary navigation/conversion destination.
- Use cards with image, category, date, title, summary, and reading time.
- Start with material already implied by the homepage: run-cycle iteration, character rebuilding, and background readability.
- Add a lightweight archive by category once there are enough entries to justify it.

### Studio

- Introduce the four team members with names, roles, and concise human biographies.
- Show how the studio works through one concrete process section, not only principles.
- Add candid workspace or development images when available. An illustrated team treatment can work if photography is not ready.
- Preserve the origin story and the “Replay / Every / Memory” language; it is strong supporting content.

### Press

- Add a single downloadable ZIP plus individual files: studio logo, game logo, screenshots, key art, team photos, boilerplate, and fact sheet.
- Present contact, embargo/release status, and asset usage guidance clearly.
- Use dated news rows for announcements and significant milestones.
- Keep the layout plain and fast; press visitors value accuracy and retrieval speed over spectacle.

## Responsive recommendations

- Reduce the mobile logo/hero media footprint so the game visual, not only the text and controls, appears in the first viewport.
- Put the primary CTA directly after the premise; move the secondary text link after the first visual.
- Shorten mobile copy aggressively. Aim for a 2–3 line premise and one compact status row.
- Keep tap targets at least 44 px and retain the strong menu labeling.
- Prefer 16:9 media, stacked cards, and edge-to-edge art on mobile to avoid narrow text-and-image splits.

## Prioritized roadmap

### P0 — before broader promotion

1. Populate the Journal with three real posts or stop using it as the primary CTA destination.
2. Create a credible game-media hero asset and a designed 16:9 trailer placeholder.
3. Replace the Trash Dash page’s text-only title with the official logo.
4. Build a usable press kit with downloadable assets and a fact sheet.

### P1 — highest design return

1. Compress the homepage from roughly ten narrative sections to six functional sections.
2. Reorder mobile content so game imagery appears in the first viewport.
3. Add team identity and process evidence to the Studio page.
4. Add three visual feature/gameplay modules to Trash Dash.

### P2 — after content cadence is established

1. Add Journal categories, archive behavior, and optional email signup.
2. Add motion with restraint: short gameplay loops, character idle animation, and subtle section transitions.
3. Add press/news history and store badges as launch infrastructure becomes available.

## Success criteria

- A first-time visitor can answer “What is the game?”, “What do I do?”, “Who is making it?”, and “What should I do next?” within one minute.
- Game or character media appears in the first desktop and mobile viewport.
- The primary CTA leads to a completed, immediately useful destination.
- The homepage contains no more than six major content sections before the footer.
- The Journal has at least three real entries before it is promoted globally.
- Press visitors can download approved assets without sending an email.
